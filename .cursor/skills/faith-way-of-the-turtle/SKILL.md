## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Trading is risk transfer, not ownership.** Investors buy companies/assets; traders buy contracts to assume liquidity risk or price risk from hedgers. Price moves when collective willingness to bid/offer changes.

**Emotion is the edge source and the failure mode.** Loss aversion, sunk costs, disposition, outcome bias, recency, anchoring, herd behavior, and belief in small samples create repeated market patterns — and destroy discretionary accounts. Name the bias to interrupt it.

**Good trading ≠ being right.** Think in probabilities and distributions. Expect many losers in trend systems. A valid plan with a 10-loss streak can still be "trading well." Detach ego from trade outcomes.

**Edge = recurring statistical advantage after costs.** Build from portfolio selection + entries + exits matched by timeframe. Measure entries with E-ratio (volatility-normalized MFE/MAE). Random entries ≈ 1.0; filtered Donchian breakouts show medium-term edge even when short-term feels adverse.

**Stay in the game.** Money management is survival art. Size with **N** (ATR) so markets contribute comparable dollar volatility. Cap aggregate risk with **unit limits**. Prefer sim drawdowns ≤ ~½ psychological tolerance. Conservative compounding beats blowup-seeking returns.

**Complete systems only.** Markets, position sizing, entries, stops, exits, tactics — missing any piece is not a system. Turtle rules: dual breakout systems, ½N pyramids, ~2N stops, opposite breakout exits, liquid-market universe.

**Backtests lie in structured ways.** Trader effects crowd patterns; luck fakes skill; optimization and curve-fitting inflate history. Prefer robust metrics (RAR%, R-cubed), representative multi-state samples, and stress tests. Build for unknowable futures with **diversity + simplicity**, not a single max-MAR champion.

**Consistency is the scarce alpha.** Dennis: publishable rules aren't enough — confidence to follow them when things go bad is. Conquer ego; eat humble pie; match style to personality.
