# Chapter 4: Think Like a Turtle

## Core Idea
Good trading is not about being right — it is about **trading right**. Think long-run probabilities; ignore the drama of individual trade outcomes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **It's not about who's right**: Prediction addiction creates hero/scum emotional swings. Turtles care about making money, not forecasting.
- **Forget the past (holistically)**: Learn from history without recency-weighted obsession.
- **Avoid the future tense**: Stop "the market will…" statements; use possibilities/probabilities.
- **Thinking in probabilities**: Distributional thinking (histograms of returns/trades) instead of point forecasts.
- **Dos and don'ts for Turtle thinking**: Consistency over cleverness; no favorites; no excuses.

## Key Concepts
- **Outcome independence**: A 10-loss streak under a valid plan can still be "trading well" (bad variance).
- **Recency bias in practice**: Skipping a market after many losers just before the big trend.
- **Character vs specifics**: Future is unknowable in specifics, foreseeable in character (trends recur because human cognition recurs).
- **Insurance analogy**: Price risk cover the way insurers price uncertain losses — sell/buy risk when odds favor you over time.
- **Skew / fat tails**: Trend systems often show elongated right tails in return histograms.

## Mental Models
- Use **probability distributions**, not narratives, to evaluate systems.
- Prefer being **wrong often** if winners are large enough (trend following profile).
- Treat "I have no idea where price goes next" as professional, not ignorance.

## Anti-patterns
- Playing favorites among markets after recent pain.
- Excuses for skipping signals ("cocoa is too risky after 17 losers").
- Judging intelligence by last trade's P&L.
- Predicting direction because peers expect you to "know."

## Worked Example
Cocoa 1998: 17 consecutive losing breakout trades totaling about −$16,750, then a large winning short starting Nov 6 that (with adds) produced ~+$55,903 overall. Friend skipped the trend due to recency bias after prior cocoa losses — exactly when the Turtle process needed consistency.

## Key Takeaways
1. Trade the process; detach ego from trade outcomes.
2. Recency bias is a silent account killer.
3. Probabilities > predictions.
4. Expect long loser streaks in trend systems.
5. Consistency beats discretionary cleverness.
