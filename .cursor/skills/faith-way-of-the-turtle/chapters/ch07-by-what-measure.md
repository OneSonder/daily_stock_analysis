# Chapter 7: By What Measure?

## Core Idea
Mature traders obsess over **risk measurement**. Industry risk/reward ratios often blind you to the risks that actually ruin accounts — drawdowns, droughts, price shocks, and system death.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four primary trading risks**: Drawdowns, low returns, price shocks, system death.
- **Risk/reward as the selection question**: Prefer highest risk-adjusted outcome — but only after defining risk honestly.
- **Giveback vs loss drawdowns**: Trend followers often give back open profits; that is different psychologically (and managerially) from a string of flat losses.
- **LTCM lesson**: Risks outside traditional measures still kill.

## Key Concepts
- **Drawdown**: Peak-to-trough equity decline; depth + duration; often underestimated via log charts.
- **Newbie trap**: Seeing 38% historical DD and thinking "I can handle that" — then quitting at ~40%+ live.
- **Low-return risk**: Erratic path to same CAGR is inferior to steady compounding path.
- **Price shock**: Sudden unhedgeable move (limit-locked markets, crash aftermath, geopolitical shock).
- **System death**: Market dynamics change so a formerly profitable edge stops working.
- **Limit-locked nightmare**: Cannot exit while losses compound day after day.

## Mental Models
- Size risk so historical DD is **≤ half** of what you think you can tolerate.
- Prefer smooth return streams when comparing equal CAGR paths.
- Monitor aggregate portfolio risk, not only per-trade stops (Dennis monitored Turtle aggregate risk).

## Anti-patterns
- Choosing systems by advertised return alone.
- Using log-scale charts to emotionally minimize drawdowns.
- Selective trading mid-drawdown "to reduce risk" (often skips the recovery winners).
- Assuming institutional-friendly low-vol trading equals Turtle-era 100%+ returns.

## Worked Example
Donchian Trend $100k test ~43.7% CAGR with ~38% historical DD. Newbie starts live; soon hits ~42% DD (worse than test). Fear/doubt → selective entries → miss winners → quit near 50% loss. Same system that looked tradable on a chart becomes untradable psychologically at aggressive risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define the risks that actually stop trading.
2. Drawdown tolerance is usually overestimated.
3. Price shocks and locked markets are existential.
4. System death is a risk class, not bad luck.
5. Honest measurement precedes honest leverage.
