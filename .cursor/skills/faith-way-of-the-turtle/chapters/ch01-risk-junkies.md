# Chapter 1: Risk Junkies

## Core Idea
Traders don't buy "things" the way investors do — they buy and sell **risk**. Markets exist to transfer risk from hedgers to traders who specialize in liquidity risk or price risk.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Investor vs Trader**: Investors buy companies/assets for long-haul ownership (Buffett model). Traders buy contracts and care primarily about **price**, not operational fundamentals.
- **Risk transfer**: Markets developed so parties can transfer risk (Bernstein). Hedgers offload price risk; traders absorb it for profit opportunity.
- **Hedging**: Buying/selling futures to offset business exposure (e.g., Southwest locking oil near $26 while spot rose past $60).

## Key Concepts
- **Liquidity risk**: Risk you cannot buy/sell when you want (no counterparty at a fair price).
- **Price risk**: Risk prices move significantly against you.
- **Spread**: Bid–ask difference; scalpers/market makers earn the spread.
- **Scalper / market maker**: Short-term liquidity provider; profits from spread, not big directional moves.
- **Arbitrage**: Exploit liquidity mismatches across related markets/venues.
- **Speculator / position trader**: Assumes price risk; profits from directional moves (including shorting).
- **Tick / minimum tick**: Smallest exchange-defined price fluctuation; contract size × tick = dollar P&L per tick.
- **Hedger / Scalper / Speculator triangle**: The three roles that interact on every meaningful futures trade.

## Mental Models
- Use **risk categories** (liquidity vs price) to classify what edge you're actually selling.
- Think of price as the **collective perception** of buyers and sellers — when willingness to trade at a price changes, price moves.
- In a panic, inexperienced traders exit late; experienced traders often reverse early and sell into late panic.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling yourself an investor while trading**: Short-horizon price gambling dressed up as long-term ownership.
- **Ignoring who is on the other side**: Not knowing whether flow is hedge, scalper, or speculative order flow.
- **Holding through floor panic without a plan**: Losses can explode in minutes when large aggressive orders hit thin liquidity.

## Worked Example
ACME hedges GBP exposure by buying 10 CME GBP contracts from scalper Sam (spread $1.8450–$1.8452). Sam tries to flatten into the other side. Speculator Mr. Ice takes the other side betting on decline. When a large broker buys aggressively, scalpers get squeezed, spread widens/prices lift, and weak shorts panic-buy — creating opportunity for disciplined traders who exited early and flipped long.

## Key Takeaways
1. Trading is risk transfer, not "buying commodities to own them."
2. Know which risk you are paid to take: liquidity or price.
3. Price moves when collective willingness to bid/offer at a price changes.
4. Panic and inexperience create opportunity for prepared counterparties.
5. Electronic markets change venue, not the psychology of hedgers/scalpers/speculators.
